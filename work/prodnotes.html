prod notes

Moving session notes from buried below the fold to always within reach — without adding a new page or breaking the operator's flow.

00

problem

Production Notes already existed in Shelfmark — but they were at the bottom of the main content column, below the inspection canvas, below the machine info card, below everything. Reaching them meant scrolling away from the live feed entirely. On a factory floor, that's enough friction to make a feature invisible. The information operators needed to log wasn't missing — ink levels, camera alignment issues, color calibration flags — it was being passed verbally or written on paper because the digital input was in the wrong place at the wrong depth.

solution

Production Notes was moved into the right panel of the Machine View as a tab alongside Recent History. Operators can now switch between their live defect feed and session notes with a single click, without leaving the canvas or losing context. The panel handles both modes — no new real estate, no new navigation layer. Each note is saved explicitly, attributed to its author, and timestamped — making the log useful across shifts and traceable for supplier dispute documentation.

The right panel already existed. The question was whether Production Notes belonged inside it — and the answer was obvious once the scrolling problem was named clearly.

The first design question was where — not how. A floating modal was considered but ruled out immediately: it would interrupt the canvas view every time an operator needed to log something. A dedicated notes page was also considered and dropped — adding navigation overhead to a task that should take seconds.

The right panel already carried Recent History, the feed of defect thumbnails logged in real time. Pairing Production Notes there as a tab made the structure legible: the panel is the operator's contextual sidebar, and both tabs belong to that mental model. The two views aren't competing — they serve different moments in the same inspection session.

The tab indicator itself went through one revision. The first version used a filled pill badge to mark the active tab. At the density of the right panel, the pill read as a clickable button rather than a selection state. Switching to an underline indicator resolved the ambiguity — it signals "you are here" rather than "press this."
